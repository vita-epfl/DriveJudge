# Structured-state validation of the cosmos-drive-dreams traffic-law labels

Generated by `src/analysis/structured_state/check_scenarios.py`.

## 0. What the structured state is

Every `cosmos-drive-dreams` clip was produced by Cosmos-Transfer1 from an HD-map **control video** rendered along an ego trajectory, on top of the NVIDIA RDS-HQ seed dataset. For the violation scenarios the author replayed the seed clip in the Cosmos-Drive-Dreams viser editor (`visualize_rds_hq.py`), dragged the ego camera through the red light / past the stop line / onto an overtaking path at a handful of keyframes, and exported the Bezier-interpolated poses to `rds_hq/novel_pose/generated/<clip_id>.tar`. The violation therefore holds **by construction**, and the edited trajectory *is* the structured state.

What is available, per seed clip:

- `pose/<clip_id>.tar` and `novel_pose/generated/<clip_id>.tar`: 4x4 **camera-to-world** matrices (OpenCV camera convention: +x right, +y down, +z forward) for 7 cameras and 297-300 frames at **30 Hz**, in a metric, clip-local `rig` world frame with z up. This analysis uses `camera_front_wide_120fov`, the camera the editor records keyframes for.
- HD-map layers `3d_wait_lines` (stop bars, with `type` in {STOP, YIELD, UNKNOWN}, `intersection_subtype` in {ENTRY, EXIT} and an `is_implicit` flag), `3d_traffic_lights` and `3d_traffic_signs` (8-corner cuboids; signs carry a `sign_class`, e.g. `TRAFFIC_SIGN_REGULATORY_R1_STOP`), `3d_lanes`, `3d_lanelines`, `3d_crosswalks`, and `all_object_info` (other agents' boxes). Every label declares `coordinate_frame: rig`, i.e. the same frame as the poses -- the toolkit projects them with `inv(pose)` and no other transform.
- **No signal state**: the traffic-light labels carry no bulb colour.

Derived quantities in `scenario_state.parquet` (one row per scenario x window): ego path (`ego_x`/`ego_y`), speed profile (`speed_ms`, m/s), the nearest wait line ahead of the path and the nearest *controlled* one (`ctl_*`), signed distance to that line per frame (`dist_to_line_m`), the crossing frame, the minimum speed within 10 m before the line, `stopped_before_line` (< 0.5 m/s for >= 0.5 s), whether a light / stop sign lies within 25 m of the line, and the signed lateral offset of the edited path w.r.t. the recorded one (+ = left), from which `overtaking_side` follows at a +/-1.5 m threshold.

## 4. How the benchmark scenario names were linked to the seed clips

`mapping.txt` uses the **author's** scenario numbering; the benchmark renumbered everything, because each seed clip yields *two* 121-frame control videos and each became its own benchmark scenario. Joining the two name spaces by string equality pairs the wrong trajectories. `scenario_map.py` recovers the link from the per-(control video, weather) captions that drove Cosmos-Transfer1: the benchmark's caption files (`final_dataset/.../captions/<scenario>_<weather>.txt`) are matched verbatim against `rendering/generated/captions_rewritten/<control video>.json`, the 8 weather variants vote, and the vote is cross-checked against the sorted render order. Full table in `scenario_video_map.json`.

| confidence | n |
| --- | --- |
| clip certain (shared caption), chunk from render order | 54 |
| clip+chunk (caption vote == render order) | 51 |
| unresolved | 42 |
| caption vote only | 42 |
| conflict (caption vote != render order) | 34 |
| clip certain, chunk from render order (caption vote disagrees) | 22 |
| render order only | 5 |

Only rows whose two sources of evidence agree on the seed clip enter the join; `conflict` rows are dropped. Within a resolved pair the *chunk* (which half of the clip) is the least certain part -- both halves share the same edited trajectory, so a chunk swap changes the frame window but never the clip.

## 5. Questions the structured state cannot settle

- `Are the traffic lights red ?` and `Is the ego car behavior consistent with visible cues of the traffic lights ?` -- the `3d_traffic_lights` labels carry only `label_name = traffic_light`; there is **no signal state** in the HD map, and Cosmos-Transfer1 paints the bulb colour freely. (22 joined scenarios carry the first question, 22 the second.)
- Anything about other agents' behaviour: `all_object_info` holds their boxes, but the labels in scope here are all ego-centric.

## 6. Three to five sentences the paper can reuse

> The cosmos-drive-dreams violation clips are not labelled post hoc: each one was rendered from an HD-map control video generated along an ego trajectory that we edited by hand in the Cosmos-Drive-Dreams trajectory editor, so the traffic-law label holds by construction. To make that auditable we re-derived, from the exported 30 Hz poses and the RDS-HQ map layers alone, the ego path, its speed profile, the signed distance to the nearest stop bar governed by a traffic light or stop sign, the crossing time, the minimum speed in the last 10 m before that line, and the signed lateral offset of the edited path with respect to the recorded one. Over the 58 seed scenarios covered by the editor log, 48 carry a hand-edited trajectory, 46 have a controlled stop bar ahead of the ego and 39 cross it, always without stopping (minimum speed in the approach zone never falls below 0.5 m/s for 0.5 s in any of them). Matched to the benchmark clips, the structured state reproduces the `following the traffic rules` label on 70/86 scenarios (81%) and the overtaking side on 14/18 (78%); 13 of the 16 rule disagreements are explained by the 121-frame render window ending before (or starting after) the violation, i.e. the label describes the scenario while the clip shows only its approach -- a labelling caveat we now document rather than a labelling error.

### The frame-window caveat, stated precisely

`render_from_rds_hq.py` cuts each 297-300-frame seed clip into non-overlapping 121-frame windows at 30 fps (`TARGET_CHUNK_FRAME = 121`, `OVERLAP_FRAME = 0`) and drops the tail, so exactly two videos survive per clip: frames 0-120 and frames 121-241. The hand-edited violation happens at one instant in the 10 s trajectory, so roughly half of the rendered windows contain only the approach or only the aftermath. In this table 36 of 116 rendered windows actually contain the crossing. Scenario-level labels were nonetheless applied to both windows, which is the single largest source of label / video mismatch in the traffic-law questions.

## 4. Author review of the disputed scenarios (2026-09-11) — what this analysis can and cannot claim

The 18 scenarios where label and trajectory disagreed were reviewed by the author on the actual clips: **16 show the
violation, 2 do not** (`cross_red_025` (6 clips), `cross_red_029` (3 clips)). So the "event outside the rendered window" explanation was wrong in 9 of 11 cases: the
recovered trajectory-chunk → clip mapping (captions-based, after the benchmark renumbering) is not reliable at the
clip level, and per-clip statements derived from it must not be used.

What the structured state does establish, at the **scenario** level: 48 of 58 mapped scenarios have a hand-edited
ego trajectory (median displacement 110 m from the recorded drive) and 39 of the 46 with a controlled stop bar cross it
without stopping — the violations were authored, not incidental. What it cannot establish: the content of a given
rendered clip. Cosmos-Transfer1 adds generation variance on top of the trajectory (a light rendered green instead of
red, the event falling outside the 4-s window, weather-specific artefacts), which is why every clip was labelled by
watching it, and why the per-clip labels — not the trajectories — are the ground truth. The traffic-light colour is
in any case not part of the structured state.

Paper framing: "violation scenarios were authored as ego trajectories in the Cosmos-Drive-Dreams editor (structured
state available for N scenarios; 39/46 cross a controlled stop bar without stopping); because the generator does not
reproduce the conditioning deterministically, each rendered clip was labelled individually from the video."
